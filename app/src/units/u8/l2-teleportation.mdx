Alice has a qubit in a state $|\psi\rangle$ she knows nothing about. Bob is in another city. There's no quantum channel between them, only a phone line. Alice needs Bob to end up with $|\psi\rangle$.

Everything you know says this should be impossible. Measuring $|\psi\rangle$ gives her one bit and destroys the rest. She can't clone it and keep trying. And a phone line carries bits, while $\theta$ and $\phi$ are continuous numbers.

## Why it works: the algebra

Write $|\psi\rangle = \alpha|0\rangle + \beta|1\rangle$. The starting state is $|\psi\rangle \otimes \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}(|00\rangle + |11\rangle)$. After Alice's CNOT ($q_0 \to q_1$) and $H$ on $q_0$, expanding and regrouping by Alice's two qubits gives

$$
\tfrac12\Big[\,|00\rangle(\alpha|0\rangle+\beta|1\rangle) + |01\rangle(\alpha|1\rangle+\beta|0\rangle) + |10\rangle(\alpha|0\rangle-\beta|1\rangle) + |11\rangle(\alpha|1\rangle-\beta|0\rangle)\Big]
$$

$$
= \tfrac12\Big[\,|00\rangle\,|\psi\rangle + |01\rangle\,X|\psi\rangle + |10\rangle\,Z|\psi\rangle + |11\rangle\,XZ|\psi\rangle\Big].
$$

That's the whole trick. CNOT followed by $H$ is the **Bell-basis measurement** circuit (the reverse of the Bell-pair maker). It sorts the joint state into four branches. In each branch $\alpha$ and $\beta$ already sit on Bob's qubit, scrambled by a Pauli that depends only on the branch label, not on $\alpha$ or $\beta$. Measuring tells Alice the label. Two bits name one of four labels, and Bob unscrambles.

<NumQuiz id="u8l2-prob" answer={0.25} tol={0.001}>
  <Q>What is the probability that Alice's measurement gives $m_1m_2 = 11$? (Does it depend on $|\psi\rangle$?)</Q>
  <Explain>Each branch has amplitude $\tfrac12$ times a *normalised* state, so every outcome has probability $\tfrac14$, **independent of $|\psi\rangle$**. That's essential. If the outcome probabilities depended on $\alpha, \beta$, Alice's bits would leak information about $|\psi\rangle$, and a measurement that learns about the state has to disturb it.</Explain>
</NumQuiz>

## Teleportation in the Circuit Lab (deferred measurement)

In a circuit you can replace "measure, phone, then classically controlled $X$/$Z$" with **controlled gates**. A CNOT from $q_1$ to $q_2$ applies $X$ exactly when $q_1 = 1$, and a CZ from $q_0$ to $q_2$ applies $Z$ when $q_0 = 1$. This is the **principle of deferred measurement**: measuring a control qubit and then classically controlling a gate gives the same statistics as using the quantum controlled gate and measuring later (or never).

Below, $q_0$ starts in a fixed "mystery" state ($\theta=\pi/3$, $\phi=\pi/4$), shown as ψ on the wire. The first four columns are already built: Bell pair, then Alice's CNOT and $H$. **Add Bob's two corrections** so that $q_2$ ends up holding $|\psi\rangle$. Watch $q_2$'s mini Bloch sphere: it should become a full-length yellow arrow matching $q_0$'s original.

<Note title="Why do Alice's qubits end in |+⟩|+⟩?">
With deferred measurement, nobody measures $q_0$ and $q_1$. After the corrections they're left in $|+\rangle|+\rangle$, an equal superposition of all four "outcomes", completely unentangled with Bob. Measuring them would give each $m_1 m_2$ with probability ¼, exactly as before. Either way, **none** of $|\psi\rangle$ remains on Alice's side.
</Note>

## What teleportation is not

<Warning>
**Teleportation doesn't send anything faster than light.** Until Alice's two bits arrive, Bob's qubit is maximally mixed from his point of view (the "what Bob can know" sphere), whatever $|\psi\rangle$ was. Only the classical bits make it useful, and they travel at light speed at most. **It doesn't clone either.** Alice's measurement leaves her qubits in $|m_1m_2\rangle$, and the original $|\psi\rangle$ is gone. At no moment do two copies exist. And no matter is transported: only a *state* moves, from one qubit onto another that was already at Bob's end.
</Warning>

  <Explain>A guess is right only ¼ of the time, and Bob can't tell when it is. Averaged over outcomes his qubit is maximally mixed until the bits arrive. <History>
Teleportation was discovered by **Charles Bennett, Gilles Brassard, Claude Crépeau, Richard Jozsa, Asher Peres and William Wootters**, published in *Physical Review Letters* in 1993. The first experiments came in 1997–98, from **Anton Zeilinger's group in Innsbruck** (Bouwmeester et al., *Nature*, with photon polarisation) and from Francesco De Martini's group in Rome. In 2017 the Chinese satellite **Micius** teleported photon states from a ground station in Tibet to orbit, over distances up to about **1,400 km**.
</History>

<CSCorner>
Teleportation is the quantum version of `mv`. It moves state between registers using a pre-shared resource and a 2-bit message. Its generalisation, **gate teleportation**, lets you apply a hard gate by teleporting through a specially prepared entangled state, and that idea underlies much of fault-tolerant quantum computing (magic-state injection) and measurement-based quantum computing. In distributed quantum computing, teleportation is how quantum data moves between processors linked only by entanglement and classical networks.
</CSCorner>

## Summary

- **Resources:** 1 shared Bell pair + 2 classical bits → 1 qubit state moved ($1\text{ ebit} + 2\text{ bits} \ge 1\text{ qubit}$).
- **Protocol:** Alice does CNOT($\psi\to$ her half), then $H$ on $\psi$, then measures both and gets $m_1m_2$ with probability ¼ each. Bob applies $X^{m_2}$ then $Z^{m_1}$.
- Key identity: $|\psi\rangle|\Phi^+\rangle = \tfrac12\sum_{m}|m_1m_2\rangle\,X^{m_2}Z^{m_1}|\psi\rangle$ after Alice's gates.
- **Deferred measurement:** the classical corrections can be replaced by CNOT and CZ gates.
- No faster-than-light signalling (Bob's qubit is random until the bits arrive) and no cloning (Alice's original is destroyed).
