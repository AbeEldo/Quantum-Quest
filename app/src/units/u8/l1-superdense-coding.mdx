## The Holevo limit: 1 bit per qubit

Alice can prepare any state $|\psi\rangle$ she likes, but Bob has one copy and no way to clone it (World 6). A measurement in any basis has two outcomes, so it gives him one bit. Choosing among more elaborate measurements doesn't help either. **Holevo's theorem (1973)** says that $n$ qubits sent without prior entanglement convey at most $n$ classical bits.

The continuum of qubit states is real, but it's **not readable**. That's the same theme as World 6's warning that $2^n$ amplitudes can't be read out.

## The protocol

The trick is to encode into a **two-qubit** space, which has room for four orthogonal states, the four Bell states. It works even though Alice touches only one of the two qubits.

1. **Beforehand:** Alice and Bob share $|\Phi^+\rangle = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}(|00\rangle + |11\rangle)$. Alice holds qubit A, Bob holds qubit B.
2. **Encode:** to send bits $b_1b_2$, Alice applies a gate to qubit A alone:

| message | Alice applies | shared state becomes |
|---|---|---|
| 00 | $I$ | $\lvert\Phi^+\rangle = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}(\lvert 00\rangle + \lvert 11\rangle)$ |
| 01 | $X$ | $\lvert\Psi^+\rangle = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}(\lvert 10\rangle + \lvert 01\rangle)$ |
| 10 | $Z$ | $\lvert\Phi^-\rangle = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}(\lvert 00\rangle - \lvert 11\rangle)$ |
| 11 | $X$, then $Z$ | $\lvert\Psi^-\rangle = \tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}(\lvert 01\rangle - \lvert 10\rangle)$ |

3. **Send:** Alice sends qubit A to Bob. **One qubit travels.**
4. **Decode:** Bob now holds both qubits. He runs the Bell-pair circuit backwards, CNOT (A controls B) then $H$ on A, which maps the four Bell states to $|00\rangle,|01\rangle,|10\rangle,|11\rangle$. He measures and reads $b_1b_2$.

## No free lunch: counting resources

So did one qubit carry 2 bits, beating Holevo? Count everything that was sent. Before the protocol, a Bell pair had to be distributed, which means a qubit travelled to Alice and another to Bob. In total **two** qubits travelled, carrying 2 bits. Holevo survives. What superdense coding does is let you send half the quantum communication **in advance**, at off-peak time, before you even know the message.

The resource accounting is written as an inequality:

$$
1\ \text{ebit} + 1\ \text{qubit sent} \;\ge\; 2\ \text{classical bits sent}.
$$

(An **ebit** is one shared Bell pair.) Teleportation, next lesson, is the mirror image: $1\ \text{ebit} + 2\ \text{bits} \ge 1\ \text{qubit}$.

<Warning>
"Superdense coding sends information faster than light, or sends 2 bits for free." **Neither.** Alice's qubit still has to physically travel to Bob, at light speed or slower. Until it arrives, Bob's qubit is maximally mixed and tells him nothing (no-signalling). And the pre-shared pair cost a qubit of transmission earlier on. Entanglement doesn't create information. It lets you **move when** the quantum communication happens.
</Warning>

## A security bonus, and the history

**Bonus:** an eavesdropper who intercepts the travelling qubit learns *nothing*. Qubit A alone is maximally mixed for every message, as you saw on the mini Bloch sphere, so the four messages look identical to anyone without qubit B.

<History>
Superdense coding was proposed by **Charles Bennett and Stephen Wiesner** in 1992 ("Communication via one- and two-particle operators on Einstein-Podolsky-Rosen states", *Phys. Rev. Lett.*). The first experiment, by **Klaus Mattle, Harald Weinfurter, Paul Kwiat and Anton Zeilinger** (Innsbruck, 1996), used polarisation-entangled photons. With only linear optics, Bob can't tell all four Bell states apart (just three outcomes are distinguishable), so they sent "trits": $\log_2 3 \approx 1.58$ bits per photon. That's still more than one bit per photon, which is the point. Full four-state Bell measurements need extra tricks such as hyperentanglement or nonlinear interactions.
</History>

<CSCorner>
Quantum Shannon theory treats ebits, qubits and classical bits like currencies with exchange rates. Superdense coding and teleportation are the two basic conversions, and they're optimal: you can't get 3 bits from an ebit plus a qubit, or teleport a qubit with only 1 bit. A whole algebra of **resource inequalities** builds on these, much as classical information theory builds on channel capacity. The entanglement-assisted classical capacity of a noiseless qubit channel is exactly 2 bits per use.
</CSCorner>

## Summary

- Without entanglement, one qubit carries at most **1 classical bit** (Holevo).
- **Superdense coding:** share $|\Phi^+\rangle$. Alice applies $I, X, Z$ or $ZX$ to her half to choose one of the four orthogonal Bell states, then sends her qubit. Bob applies CNOT then $H$ and measures, giving **2 bits**.
- The message lives in the *correlation* (including the relative phase) between the qubits. Qubit A alone is maximally mixed, so an interceptor learns nothing.
- Resource count: $1$ ebit $+ 1$ qubit $\ge 2$ bits. Nothing travels faster than light, and nothing comes for free.
